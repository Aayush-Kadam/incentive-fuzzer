# M6 Hostile Review

## Verdict

**PASS WITH LIMITATIONS.** M6 demonstrates a disciplined repair-regression workflow, but candidate discovery is mostly transparent brute-force tuning plus one obvious smoothing transform. Its value is stronger in rejecting false fixes than in inventing mechanisms.

## Is this merely brute-force parameter tuning?

Largely yes. Parameter repair exhausts small author-chosen grids. The structural transformer recognizes one hard-cutoff form and generates linear phase-outs. This is reproducible and auditable, not algorithmically novel synthesis.

## Does counterexample guidance add value?

It adds boundary-aligned re-attack and prevents threshold shifts from appearing successful merely because the old witness location is clean. It does not yet learn new repair families from arbitrary counterexamples.

## Are objectives chosen to make preferred repairs win?

They are self-authored and normative. M6 reduces this risk by publishing every dimension and keeping two scholarship and two strategic-game candidates on their Pareto frontiers. Different constraints or state domains can change the frontier.

## Are policy tradeoffs hidden?

No single repair score is used. The scholarship frontier exposes a direct tradeoff: the shorter phase-out has lower distance and fiscal deviation but 0.4 local fragility; the wider one has zero observed fragility but higher fiscal deviation. Nevertheless, the fiscal metric is synthetic and domain-specific.

## Does the engine overfit the frozen attack suite?

Possibly. M2 boundary-aligned search, M3 UNSAT, M4 frozen populations, M5 exact equilibrium checks, and mutation stress reduce but cannot eliminate overfitting. Historical and external attacks are absent.

## Can a repair pass one layer and fail another?

Yes. The registered enforcement-rebate candidate changes isolated gain from +1 to 0 but also raises the interaction bonus from 3 to 6 against cost 5, creating `(MANIPULATE,MANIPULATE)`. It is rejected as `REPAIR_INDUCED_VULNERABILITY`. The fixture is intentionally constructed and therefore validates logic rather than prevalence.

## Are trivial repairs rejected?

Yes. Zero scholarship award removes the exploit but fails the benefit-coverage constraint. Zero procurement process cost similarly violates the preserved-purpose constraint. Constraint adequacy still depends on human specification.

## Does repair add complexity without benefit?

The scholarship structural repair adds a three-segment schedule and fiscal change. The grid cannot establish that this administrative complexity is justified. It only exposes the tradeoff.

## Are Pareto frontiers meaningful?

They are mathematically correct for declared exact metrics. They are not social-welfare frontiers and should not be read as recommendations.

## Are equilibrium regressions exact?

Yes for the supplied finite pure-action games. Game candidates are exhaustively enumerated. Mixed, dynamic, Bayesian, and coalition responses remain outside coverage.

## Does mutation testing expose fragility?

Yes: 245 registered mutations produced nonzero fragility for some otherwise passing repairs. The neighborhood is tiny and author-chosen, so zero observed fragility is not a robustness proof.

## Strongest surviving criticism

All mechanisms, repair grids, constraints, populations, games, and mutation neighborhoods remain self-authored and synthetic. M6 can prevent false confidence relative to its declared suite, but it cannot establish that the suite captures real policy objectives or attack behavior.

## Unresolved critical conceptual error

None within the bounded deterministic scope. The principal limitation is external validity and normative specification, not internal repair-gate soundness.
